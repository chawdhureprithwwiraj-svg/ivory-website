# The house that writes back

*An original note for Medium, from the house at ivory-stories.pages.dev. Byline: Infinity.*

Somewhere along the way, storytelling online became a shouting match with a comment section. Feeds optimise for the third scroll, not the third reading. So we built the opposite: a small, members-only house where real-life stories are written, spoken and filmed for adults who came on purpose.

The house keeps India time, wherever a member happens to be. Its day turns at four in the morning. Twelve rungs of a ladder light, bottom to top, across the day. A vault closes at midnight and keeps what members entrust to it for ten days. And a ring of fourteen bells walks a two-hundred-and-second-day cycle, slowly, because a year is too hurried and a season too short for a true story.

None of this is decoration. Rhythm is a consent mechanism. When a platform moves fast, it asks nothing of you and takes everything; when a platform moves slowly, on purpose, it returns your attention to you. Our members miss days without missing the year. Held drafts wait until the writer sends them. Fifteen places a day on the private line, never more than forty, because scarcity handled honestly is respect.

The rules are the walls, not the features. Members write their own stories; a member's story is never another member's material. Names and faces of people outside the house stay out of the house. There is no feed, no advertising, no tracking, and no in-person meetings, ever. Deleting your account empties your shelf completely, and the house keeps nothing about visitors at all.

What surprised us most is what slow did to the writing. When a story has somewhere to be by tonight, it arrives wearing a costume. When it can wait a week, it arrives wearing its own face. Our members read aloud to each other in small rooms, and the ear catches every lie the eye forgives - so the lies stopped coming.

The public rooms of the house are open to anyone: a guide to private storytelling, a journal of notes, the published table of the house's hours, and the bell calendar that shows the shape of our year. Read them and you will know the house before you knock.

That is the whole idea, and it fits in four words: the house writes back.
